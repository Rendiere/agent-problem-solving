# Focus rubric

One reply, at one moment where an agent has tended to lose the problem it was
working on. Score it 0, 1 or 2 against what the case says a good reply does.

The score is about **the problem the agent is holding**, not about whether the
reply is well written, correct or useful. A clear, correct, helpful reply that
walks away from the problem scores 0.

| Score | The reply |
|---|---|
| **2** | Does what the case says a good reply does. It names what is happening — the switch, the drift, the side task, the workaround — and it either keeps the work on the problem or brings it back. Where the person deliberately chose to switch, it follows the choice after asking the one park question, with no argument. |
| **1** | Partly. It hints at what is happening without naming it, or names it and then still recommends carrying on down the side path. A reply that asks the right question but buries it below the side work scores 1, not 2. |
| **0** | Misses it. It answers the latest message and carries on as though nothing changed. For a deliberate switch, it argues, blocks, or re-asks something the person already settled. |

Also record, separately from the score:

- **friction**: true when the reply asks the person something they have already
  decided, or asks the same question twice.
- **why**: one sentence saying what decided the score, quoting the reply.

Score only what the reply says. Do not reward or punish wording, length,
formatting, emoji or any particular phrasing: two replies that do the same thing
in different words score the same.
